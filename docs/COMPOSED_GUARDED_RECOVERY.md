# Composed guarded recovery

`FileOversight::open_guarded(directory, profile, requirements)` recovers one existing authority domain and returns its separately held human reviewer, optional identity observer and optional policy governor together. Previously the specialized identity and campaign reopen methods each exposed only one of the latter roles; reopening again fenced the owner and invalidated the first role. This entry point uses one exclusive Store and one original recovery fence, not another authority or a mutable role getter.

`FileGuardSet` pins the exact stream, file-source, identity, campaign and nonsecret credential contracts. Every `None` means the corresponding gate must be absent, not that on-disk configuration may be accepted unchecked. First-publication revalidation is always mandatory. `FileRecoveryRequirements` additionally pins the effective exact policy, credential generation and terminal-revocation state, plus independently retained minimum journal revision, control sequence and authority epoch. The full canonical image is decoded and semantically replayed before all comparisons; all comparisons precede canonical cleanup and recovery writes. A mismatch cannot remove staged evidence or append a fence.

Only an acknowledged original fence returns the owner and role bundle. It retains original liabilities, published outcomes, sequence floors, policy history and incidents, while withdrawing old source/identity/clock eligibility and approvals. The new observer can collect fresh measurements and the new governor can approve a fresh full-history campaign on that same recovered owner. Neither role restores an effect key, lost campaign approval or provider credential. Split the returned roles between their separate custodians. Credential pairs and current file-source observations must still be provisioned through the existing interfaces.

The existing specialized and legacy entry points are unchanged and do not acquire these additional contract checks. This is opt-in filesystem reference functionality for the composed recovery obligations in plan sections 7.10, 8.8, 16.4 and 22.7; it does not qualify production recovery. Protected external floors can reject older counters, but cannot detect an equal-counter fork, a fabricated history, or rollback of the floors themselves. Identity/campaign role custody, path ancestors and canonical-file authenticity remain operator assumptions.

Seven public integration regressions cover both recovered roles through fresh identity, policy promotion and two-key publication; old key/role rejection; every optional gate; retained policy and credential revocation; each external floor; unresolved dispatch accounting; explicit legacy refusal; and corrupt suffix/profile handling. Two unit tests cover all five original Store fence replacement barriers and a syntactically valid but semantically invalid suffix. Two compile-fail doctests protect role non-cloning and the absence of a live-owner role getter. These are source tests, not reported passing executions. Rust compilation, rustfmt, Clippy, tests and doctests remain unexecuted; the editing environment lacks RCH. No Beads, qualified counts, original assertions, dependencies or journal formats were changed.

The guard inventory also pins the exact `FileDecoderConfig`, including its immutable parameter, monitor and sampler inputs. Unexpected or duplicate numerical configuration is refused before token replay; the normal semantic replay still checks every numerical witness. The original recovery fence leaves the numerical owner paused. Explicit fresh-clock resume and newly measured identity are required independently; obtaining all roles does not release old outputs or restore effect keys. Two additional integration regressions cover combined numerical continuation through two-key publication, changed monitor configuration, unexpected presence/absence, and untouched staged data on refusal.
