# Recover canonical shutdown evidence without recovering authority

`FileShutdownCampaign::inspect_canonical` resolves a domain's current canonical history beside a healthy, locked or faulted owner. Its consumer is the existing cooperative shutdown campaign after a missing or ambiguous acknowledgment. It reads exactly the registered directory and bootstrap, checks the retained whole-history prefix, and replays the complete original machine before publishing an observation. It does not acquire a writer lock, remove staged files, append a fence, refresh clocks, recover roles or issue a stop.

The inspection distinguishes an uncommitted stop, a canonical stop whose acknowledgment was lost, and a separately completed drain. `FileShutdownObservation::last_drain` retains the original sweep's per-effect outcomes and its own journal revision; a later observation does not rewrite when those results were obtained. The original sweep reports executed, confirmed-not-executed, retention-expired and refused obligations. No counter comparison or missing endpoint record is converted into nonexecution. Existing executed charges and unresolved liabilities retain their original meanings.

Every successful owner visit or canonical inspection advances an exact retained prefix. A shorter image or valid equal-counter fork cannot erase that already observed history within the campaign. A valid registered prefix cannot conceal a malformed or semantically impossible later suffix. Before historical execution, newly supplied decoder or predictor enable records outside the verified prefix are rejected. Registrations should therefore follow the intended numerical bootstrap, or a live acknowledged visit must establish the newer configuration before passive inspection. This is an exact local consistency check, not authentication of operator storage.

A report always retains all planned domains. Canonical evidence is explicitly tagged `CanonicalImage`, not relabelled as a writer acknowledgment, simultaneous fleet cut, or proof that an offline process is physically halted. Another operation's valid stop remains visible but does not satisfy this operation. Missing/inaccessible storage invalidates the latest-visit success for that member without preventing work on other members. Prior observations and all entered attempts remain in the current campaign's report.

`advance_supervised` connects the campaign to the original `FileActorSupervisor<FileOversight>` without exposing the owner to its actor port. It uses the existing supervisor borrow and therefore withdraws an unused admission snapshot just like any other privileged mutation. A spent campaign refuses before taking that borrow. The original request book drives actor-facing knowledge: a published request remains outcome-unknown after the stop, and becomes known-executed only when the original drain reconciles its receipt. Neither the campaign nor a status report can issue effect keys.

A separately retained `FileShutdownPlan` can start a new observation session and inspect each canonical domain without repeating its stop. Such a new session starts with no successful observations; only current inspections establish them. The plan and campaign visit log are not serialized by this change. Losing an old campaign also loses its newer comparison frontier and visit log unless the operator retains that object. Attempt budgets are per campaign instance, not a durable global retry allowance; no claim of whole-process coordinator recovery or cross-instance anti-rollback follows.

Six public integration regressions cover staged-write failure beside a faulted owner with an already published effect, rollback of an observed stop, reconstruction from a retained plan, a conflicting valid stop, numerical-configuration preflight and the actual actor supervisor/ticket path. Three unit regressions add ten original Store barriers across stop and drain, a semantically valid equal-counter fork, an invalid suffix and stopping while source acquisition is interrupted. Positive native drains accompany refusals. These are added assertions, not passing execution evidence or hardware power-cut tests.

The required RCH command exited 127 because `rch` is absent, before compilation. Compilation, formatting, Clippy, tests and doctests remain unexecuted. Existing tests, journal encodings, dependency policy, Beads and qualification gates are unchanged. This advances the practical multi-domain terminal-shutdown connection while leaving the full FA-120 distributed ordering, lease, authentication and qualification obligations open.
